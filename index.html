Organic Electrochemical Transistor (OECT) — 2D Device Simulation

Developed a 2D finite-element simulation of an OECT device using COMSOL Multiphysics 6.3. The Electrostatics and Transport of Diluted Species physics modules were coupled to model the mixed ionic-electronic conduction in the organic semiconductor channel. Ion transport and redistribution within the electrolyte were governed by the Nernst–Planck equation, capturing both diffusion and electromigration of mobile ions. The electrostatic potential profile across the device was solved using Poisson's equation, enabling accurate modeling of the electric double layer (EDL) at the electrolyte–channel interface and its influence on channel doping and drain current modulation.

Tools: COMSOL Multiphysics 6.3 (Electrostatics, Transport of Diluted Species)

Outcome: Generated transfer and output characteristic curves consistent with experimental OECT literature, demonstrating effective gate-voltage-controlled ion injection and volumetric doping modulation for biosensor transducer design.

📱 Wearable Technology

Wearable Biosensors: Tiny Devices, Big Impact

Published: October 18, 2025 | By Udhay Chowdhury

Imagine this: You're at a rural health clinic in Bangladesh. There's no lab, no specialist, no waiting days for blood test results. But there's a small device — the size of a credit card — that can detect bacteria in your blood within minutes. That's the promise of wearable biosensors.

What Are Wearable Biosensors?

Biosensors are analytical devices that detect biological or chemical signals and convert them into measurable electrical outputs. When integrated into wearable form factors — smartwatches, patches, rings, or even tattoos — they enable continuous, non-invasive health monitoring in real time.

Unlike traditional lab-based diagnostics that require blood draws and specialized equipment, wearable biosensors can analyze sweat, interstitial fluid, or skin contact to detect biomarkers for glucose, lactate, uric acid, cortisol, and even bacterial infections.

Why Do They Matter?

Healthcare Access: In low-resource settings like rural Bangladesh, access to diagnostic labs is limited. Wearable biosensors could decentralize healthcare, enabling point-of-care testing at home or in community clinics.

Continuous Monitoring: For patients with diabetes, heart disease, or infections, real-time monitoring can catch dangerous changes before they become emergencies. A glucose-monitoring patch, for instance, can alert a diabetic patient instantly when blood sugar spikes.

Preventive Healthcare: By tracking biomarkers continuously, wearable biosensors shift medicine from reactive (treating illness) to proactive (preventing it).

Current Research Landscape (2024-2025)

The global wearable biosensor market was valued at USD 1.5 billion in 2023 and is projected to reach USD 4.2 billion by 2030 (Grand View Research, 2024). Key research areas include:

Electrochemical Sensors: Using oxidation-reduction reactions to detect specific biomarkers in sweat. Companies like Epicore Biosystems are commercializing sweat-based biosensor patches.

Smart Textiles: Researchers are embedding sensors directly into clothing — imagine a shirt that monitors your health while you sleep!

The Challenges

Of course, there are hurdles: accuracy validation, battery life, privacy concerns, and cost. But with investment flooding in — major tech companies (Apple, Google, Samsung) and startups are racing to innovate — these challenges are being solved faster than you'd think.

What's Next?

The future? AI-powered wearables that predict health events days before they happen. Imagine your device telling you to see a doctor because it detected a pattern only AI could spot. It's coming sooner than you think.

The bottom line: Wearable biosensors are shifting healthcare from reactive (treating illness) to proactive (preventing it). And we're just getting started.

References: Global Wearable Biosensor Market Report 2024 | WHO Digital Health Strategy | IEEE Journal of Biomedical Engineering, 2024

☀️ Solar Energy

Perovskite Solar Cells: The Future of Solar Power?

Published: November 5, 2025 | By Udhay Chowdhury

Here's a mind-bending fact: Traditional silicon solar panels took 50+ years to reach 22% efficiency. Perovskite solar cells did it in 15 years. And they're still improving. Fast.

What's Perovskite?

Perovskite is a mineral with a special crystal structure. In the 2010s, scientists discovered that certain perovskite materials absorb light incredibly well and convert it to electricity. Today's perovskite solar cells reach 25.7% efficiency in labs — competing with traditional silicon!

But here's the best part: they're cheap, flexible, and easy to manufacture.

Why Should You Care?

Cost: Current silicon panels cost ~$1-2 per watt. Perovskites could drop to $0.50 per watt. That means affordable solar for everyone.

Flexibility: Unlike rigid silicon panels, perovskites can be printed on plastic, paper, or flexible substrates. Imagine solar windows or solar-powered clothing!

Light-Weight: For rooftops, boats, or drones where weight matters, perovskites are a game-changer.

Speed of Innovation: The technology is advancing at breakneck speed. Every year brings breakthroughs in efficiency and stability.

Current Status (2024-2025)

Lab Records: Oxford University achieved 27% efficiency in 2024. Companies like Perovskite Photovoltaics and Heliatek are moving toward commercial production.

Real-World Deployment: First commercial perovskite solar panels are hitting the market. Small-scale projects in Europe and Asia are proving the technology works at scale.

Timeline: Experts predict perovskite solar will capture 10% of the global market by 2030 and 25% by 2040.

The Catch: Stability

Traditional silicon panels last 25+ years. Current perovskites last 10-15 years. But scientists are solving this. Better materials, protective coatings, and hybrid designs are extending lifespan rapidly.

The Bottom Line

Perovskite solar isn't the future anymore — it's happening now. Within a decade, you might be buying solar panels at a fraction of today's cost, sticking them on your windows, and powering your home. And that's just the beginning.

References: Oxford University Perovskite Research 2024 | Nature Energy Journal 2024 | International Technology Roadmap for Photovoltaics 2025

💭 Mental Wellness

The Silent Struggle: Mental Health in Engineering

Published: December 15, 2024 | By Udhay Chowdhury

It was 2 AM on a Tuesday. I was sitting in the library, surrounded by four empty coffee cups, staring at a thermodynamics problem that made no sense. Around me, other engineering students looked equally miserable. Nobody was talking. Just the sound of pages turning and keyboards clicking.

Nobody was smiling.

That's when it hit me — engineering school isn't just academically intense. It takes a toll on your mental health in ways people don't talk about.

The Numbers Don't Lie

According to the American Psychological Association (2023), engineering students report higher rates of anxiety and depression than their peers in other disciplines. Here's why:

  • High Workload: Labs, projects, problem sets, exams — it never stops
  • Perfectionism: One mistake in a design = whole project fails
  • Imposter Syndrome: "Everyone else is smarter than me"
  • Job Market Pressure: Need internships, good GPA, projects for resume
  • Social Isolation: Everyone's studying; nobody has time to hang out

My Personal Story

During my third year, I hit a wall. I was juggling coursework, a research project, organizing events, and trying to maintain a good GPA. I thought I was managing fine. But then I couldn't sleep. I'd lie awake at 3 AM thinking about an exam three weeks away.

Then came the panic attacks. During a laboratory exam, my hands started shaking. My heart was racing. I couldn't focus. I left the exam hall and sat outside for 20 minutes just breathing.

That's when I realized: I wasn't alone. And I needed help.

What Helped Me

1. Talking to Someone: I spoke with my university's counselor. Just saying things out loud made a difference. I wasn't "weak" — I was human.

2. Reframing Perfectionism: A 90% is still an A. A good-enough project is better than no project. Done > Perfect.

3. Exercise & Sleep: Sounds cliché, but a 30-minute run cleared my head more than any all-nighter ever did.

4. Taking Breaks: I started taking real breaks — not scrolling on my phone, but actually stepping away. Reading, gaming, hanging with friends. My grades actually improved.
